# Words for building device names. Two sources, for two measured defects.
#
# 1. Every content word that appears on the *negative* side of the corpus,
#    written "word<TAB>count" with the count being how often the negatives use
#    it. A token that only ever occurs in refusals becomes a decision rule:
#    "coffee" appeared 2,080 times in negatives and zero times in a positive,
#    and "check coffee maker state" came back <unknown> on every seed. Words
#    that worked -- lamp, fan, relay, laser -- were all 75-90%% positive.
#
#    The count is here because breadth alone does not fix this. Spread evenly
#    over 7,000 words, "coffee" appears twice in 40,000 generated names and
#    stays 1000:1 one-sided. names.py samples these in proportion to the count
#    so that the words the negatives lean on hardest are also the ones most
#    likely to be a name.
#
# 2. Ordinary English (3-8 letters, lowercase) sampled deterministically from
#    the Unix /usr/share/dict/words -- Webster's Second International, public
#    domain -- with no count. This covers the other defect: words absent from
#    the corpus entirely ("cutter", "exhaust", "tank"), where the model
#    truncated the span at the last noun it recognised and returned "laser"
#    for "laser cutter". Vendored rather than read from /usr/share/dict at
#    build time, which is absent on many Linux installs.
#
# Regenerate if the negative banks change materially.
# NOT sourced from any eval set -- that is where the failure lives, and mining
# it would be training on the test set.
